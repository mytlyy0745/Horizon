### [When in doubt, flail about](https://seangoedecke.com/when-in-doubt-flail-about/) ⭐️ 7.0/10

rss · Sean Goedecke · Nov 23, 00:00

**「Background」** When software engineers face unclear situations with no guidance, caution and waiting for complete understanding are usually inefficient. Instead of getting stuck, the author argues that deliberately trying many different things helps cut through ambiguity.

**「Solution」** Effective flailing relies on trying multiple ideas, paying close attention to feedback, and ruthlessly dropping initiatives that fail to get positive results. Because most initial plans will naturally miss the mark, attempting ten different approaches rather than just one increases the odds of stumbling upon a viable path. The author categorizes this technique across three distinct domains: product flailing for discovering product-market fit through rapid feature iteration, engineering flailing for navigating complex codebases via speculative changes, and organizational flailing for resolving team friction by testing various interventions. While this iterative method feels messy and uncomfortable from the inside, it serves as a powerful engine for discovery when guided by strict responsiveness to reality.

**「Takeaway」** Embracing the discomfort of rapid, experimental trial-and-error is often the fastest way to navigate complex technical and organizational ambiguity. Pushing through the psychological barrier of feeling lost turns uncertainty into a positive signal for discovery.

### [To Code Well, Avoid Catastrophic Blunders](https://seangoedecke.com/to-code-well-avoid-blunders/) ⭐️ 7.0/10

rss · Sean Goedecke · Nov 22, 00:00

**「Background」** Engineers often approach software development by searching for brilliant tactical insights or clever abstractions, treating programming like a pursuit of winning plays. However, author Sean Goedecke argues that large commercial codebases do not reward durable advantages; instead, a single catastrophic mistake can instantly degrade an elegant system.

**「Solution」** The author contends that the secret to writing great code is treating programming like chess or tennis, where avoiding major blunders—such as hanging pieces or missing routine returns—matters far more than finding brilliant combinations. In commercial engineering, cardinal sins include introducing parallel systems, creating secondary sources of truth, complicating key interfaces, and adding unnecessary requirements that trigger combinatorial complexity and linger indefinitely. To counter this, developers must prioritize fitting tasks naturally into the existing codebase and paring requirements down to the bare minimum, viewing any urge to use clever patterns as a warning sign of impending distraction.

**「Takeaway」** Great programming in large teams does not require cleverness and often benefits from suppressing it in favor of relentless consistency and minimalism. By maintaining strict attention to detail and avoiding avoidable blunders, engineers can successfully navigate the complexities of impure commercial codebases.
